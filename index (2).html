01 — Semiconductors / AI infrastructure

Nvidia: The moat that's being mined

Nvidia's four biggest customers supply ~61% of its revenue — and each one is simultaneously building the chips meant to replace it. This is digital transformation as a defensive agility move: four hyperscalers restructuring their own decision-making (build vs. buy) to escape single-vendor risk.

Now pairs Trainium with 3rd-party silicon for inference in its own data centres — the first non-Amazon-built chip deployed at scale inside its own infrastructure.
Google ＋
TPU (Ironwood)
Longest-running in-house chip programme of the four — roughly a decade of iteration, giving it the most mature substitute for Nvidia hardware.
Microsoft ＋
Maia
Earliest-stage of the four programmes. Pressure comes from two directions — its own chip ambitions, and OpenAI (its largest AI tenant) separately building chips too.
Meta ＋
MTIA
Signed a multi-gigawatt deal with a second merchant chip supplier in 2026 — the first major hyperscaler crack toward using someone other than Nvidia at scale.
Reading it through the dissertation lens Each hyperscaler is using digital transformation (in-house silicon) to increase organisational agility (reduce single-supplier dependency) and change a core decision (build vs. buy). Nvidia's CUDA lock-in is the only thing slowing that shift down — the open question is how many years that buys.
