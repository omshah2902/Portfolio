02 — Consumer goods / Corporate restructuringLatest

Unilever: the spinoff that was supposed to prove a point

For years, one big investor kept telling Unilever the same thing: you're trying to do too much. Soap, shampoo, tea, mayonnaise, ice cream — all under one roof, all slowing each other down. So in 2024, Unilever agreed. It announced it would cut ice cream loose into its own separate company, and cut up to 7,500 office jobs while it was at it. The promise: both halves would come out leaner and faster.

In December 2025, the split finally happened. The new ice cream company — now home to Ben & Jerry's and Magnum — went public on its own for the first time. Six weeks later, it delivered its first earnings report as an independent business. It missed on profit. It missed on cash flow. The share price fell, and investors who'd been handed free shares in the split rushed to sell them.

But here's the twist that makes this more than "a spinoff had a rough start": buried in the original 2000 deal where Unilever first bought Ben & Jerry's is a clause that still applies today — an independent board, not the new company itself, controls Ben & Jerry's activism and identity. So even after being "set free," part of this business still isn't fully in charge of its own decisions.

Reading it through the dissertation lens This is the more honest half of the digital-transformation-and-agility story: the theory says restructuring should increase focus and speed of decision-making. In practice, the transformation itself came with real costs, a rocky market reception, and a governance structure inherited from decades ago that constrains the very independence the spinoff was meant to deliver. Agility isn't just a decision to restructure — it's whether the new structure can actually move freely afterward.

01 — Semiconductors / AI infrastructure

Nvidia: The moat that's being mined

Nvidia's four biggest customers supply ~61% of its revenue — and each one is simultaneously building the chips meant to replace it. This is digital transformation as a defensive agility move: four hyperscalers restructuring their own decision-making (build vs. buy) to escape single-vendor risk.

Reading it through the dissertation lens Each hyperscaler is using digital transformation (in-house silicon) to increase organisational agility (reduce single-supplier dependency) and change a core decision (build vs. buy). Nvidia's CUDA lock-in is the only thing slowing that shift down — the open question is how many years that buys.
